It started in an oil drum

Prof. Juferi Idris started by charring landscape waste in a steel oil drum. A decade later, the question is whether biochar compost can be sold by the tonne.

Lead
Prof. Ir. Dr. Juferi Idris
Years
2016 to 2024
Feedstock
Landscape waste
Coconut shell
Vetiver grass
Grants
5 biochar grants, RM 0.4M

Biochar is biomass heated with little or no air until it becomes a stable, porous carbon. Mixed into soil or compost it holds water and nutrients. Made carefully it becomes activated carbon, which sells for far more.

Prof. Ir. Dr. Juferi Idris began in 2016 with a modest grant to make biochar from landscape waste in a pilot reactor built from a steel oil drum, using the heat of the process itself to keep it going. In 2020 a larger grant scaled the same self-sustained carbonisation to coconut shell and other biomass, adding steam activation to make activated carbon. In 2021 vetiver grass became a slow-release biochar fertiliser for oil palm seedlings, with the leftover ash going to hydroponic vegetables.

2016a steel oil drum and a bag of landscape waste

Since 2023 the work has moved closer to the market, with two grants on composting with biochar and preparing it for commercial scale.

Elsewhere in the group, Dr Azil blends coconut shell biochar with xerogel to strip H2S from gas, and Prof. Syed Shatir turns sewage sludge into hydrochar fuel.
